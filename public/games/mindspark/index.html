About MindSpark: a free game for ages 6-12

MindSpark is a 50-level brain game with a friendly robot guide. Kids solve visual Aha! puzzles across three labs: a chalkboard Math Lab, an inventor's Physics Workshop and a bubbly Chemistry Lab. Every level teaches one real idea, from minus signs made of a single line to torque, reflection, displacement, pH, states of matter and electric circuits.

The puzzles are grouped into three labs. In the Math Lab, children move matchsticks and chalk lines to fix equations, count squares hidden inside grids and turn number cards upside down. The Physics Lab has see-saws, laser mirrors, gears and belts, and a water tank that shows displacement. The Chemistry Lab covers cabbage-juice pH colours, ice, water and steam, conductors and insulators, and safe kitchen reactions. Wrong tries are shown and explained, and every solved level ends with a short Brain Power fact.

How to play

Read the task, then drag or tap pieces to change the puzzle. Matchstick levels allow one or two moves; wrong tries are explained and put back. Physics and chemistry levels react straight away, like a real experiment. Stuck? The lightbulb gives three hints: a glow, a clue, and a dotted ghost of the answer.

Controls

Use a finger or a mouse. Drag a piece onto a glowing spot, or tap the piece and then tap the spot; pieces snap into place. Tap mirrors, belts, cards, bottles and chambers to change them. The Hint button gives three steps: a glow on what to change, a clue, and a dotted ghost of the move. Reset starts the level again.

Skills kids practise

  • Lateral thinking and number sense
  • Levers, light reflection, gears and buoyancy
  • Acids, bases and the pH scale
  • Ice, water and steam
  • Conductors and insulators
  • Testing ideas and learning from mistakes

For parents and teachers

Many of these puzzles can be tried at home: matchstick sums with toothpicks, a ruler see-saw with coins, red cabbage juice with lemon and baking soda, or vinegar and baking soda in a bottle. Please do kitchen experiments together, and keep the hydrogen peroxide demo for adults. Ask your child to explain why their answer works, because explaining builds real understanding.
