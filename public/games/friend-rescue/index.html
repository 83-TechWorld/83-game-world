About Friend Rescue Quest: a free game for ages 6+

Friend Rescue Quest is a bright cartoon platform adventure. The Gloop King has locked 12 friends in cages, and the hero runs, jumps, swims and climbs through meadows, lava fields, jungles, lagoons, caves, sky islands, a sea temple and a castle to set them free. Players type in the hero's name and every friend's name, and each rescued friend joins the squad, follows the hero and brings a new power.

There are 12 hand-built levels. Each one adds something new: lava steps with moving lifts, gates opened by switches and levers, crates to push, vines to climb, underwater swimming, a tall volcano climb, floating sky islands and a final boss fight with the Gloop King. Puzzles often need the squad: one friend waits on a switch while the hero runs through the gate it opens. Every level was checked with a computer solver to make sure all jumps, keys and gems can be reached.

How to play

Reach the cage at the end of each level and open it, finding the key first when the cage has a lock. Then run to the rainbow portal with your new friend. Bop goofy gloops on the head, punch them, or trap them in bubbles. Friends give powers: Double Jump, Friend Helper to hold switches, Super Punch for cracked rocks and Leaf Glide.

Controls

On a phone or tablet, use the ◀ ▶ buttons to run, ⤒ to jump, climb vines and swim, ▼ to climb down, 👊 to punch or shoot bubbles and 🤝 to make a friend wait on a switch. On a computer, use the arrow keys or A and D to run, Space or the up arrow to jump, X to punch, F for the friend helper and P to pause.

Skills kids practise

  • Timing and hand-eye coordination
  • Planning routes and solving switch puzzles
  • Teamwork: using each friend's power
  • Persistence: trying again from the last flag

For parents and teachers

This is a cartoon action game for ages 6 and up. Enemies are goofy slime monsters that pop into sparkles or float away in bubbles, with no blood or gore. Falling into lava or thorns only sends the hero back to the last flag, and there is no game over. Let your child type in the names of family and friends to rescue, then ask them which friend power helped most and why.
