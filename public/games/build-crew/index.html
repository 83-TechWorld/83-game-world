About Build Crew: a free game for ages 5-10

Build Crew turns children into junior builders. Captain Leo and Captain Maya show a blueprint, the crew builds it piece by piece, and then a 2-minute disaster check tests the building with rain, wind, water pressure, power cuts and fire drills. Twenty projects go from a treehouse to a whole eco-smart town.

Each level follows the same three steps a real building team uses: read the plan, build it, then test it. Early levels keep the blueprint on screen, while later ones hide it after a short look, so children practise visual memory. Look-alike pieces such as flat and sloped roofs, plain and sealed windows, or steps and ramps make children think about why a piece is needed, not just where it goes. When a test finds a loophole, X-ray mode shows the exact spot and the captain explains the reason.

How to play

Listen to the captains and study the blueprint. Pick a piece in the toolbox and tap or drag on the grid to build. Choose the right look-alike pieces, such as sealed windows or ramps. Press Test Build to start the disaster check. If something fails, X-ray mode shows the loophole in red, so fix it and test again.

Controls

Use a finger or a mouse. Pick a piece in the toolbox, then tap a square on the grid, or drag across several squares to build a row. Tap a placed piece again to remove it, or use the eraser. The Decorate tab has paints and stickers. Press Test Build when you are ready. The speaker button controls sound, and the Levels button opens the map.

Skills kids practise

  • Visual memory: rebuild the blueprint after it hides
  • Why buildings need foundations, pillars and sloped roofs
  • Water and electricity safety
  • Safe exits, ramps and clear paths
  • Problem solving: find and fix the loophole

For parents and teachers

Ask your child to explain a rule before testing, for example why a roof has two slopes or why wires stay away from water. Point out the same ideas around your home and town: drains in the road, ramps at shop doors and fire exits at school. A failed test is part of the fun, so praise the fix rather than the first try.
