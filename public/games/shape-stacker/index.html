About Shape Stacker: a free game for ages 6-12

Shape Stacker is a falling-shapes puzzle that teaches geometry. Shapes drop onto a graph-paper board, and children turn and slide them so they fit together. When every square in a row is filled, the row clears. Each shape is named as it appears with a quick fact, such as a hexagon having 6 sides, and new shapes join a collection of 32.

The board is graph paper, and every square on it can be cut into triangle quarters. That is why slanted shapes fit: two right triangles join into one square, a diamond fills the gaps around four squares, and a hexagon is a rectangle with triangle ends. Children see these relationships every time two pieces lock together and the game calls out a perfect fit. Round shapes and 3D solids cannot fill a grid, so they arrive packed in crates, and level 13 shows cubes, pyramids and prisms as flat nets with dashed fold lines.

How to play

Move and turn each falling shape before it lands. Fill a whole row to clear it, and clear the number of rows shown to finish the level. Triangle gaps left in a full row are filled in gold, but matching the halves exactly earns a Perfect row bonus. Keep the stack low for three stars.

Controls

On a phone or tablet, drag sideways to move, tap the board to turn, drag down to go faster and flick down to drop. The big buttons under the board do the same. On a computer, use the arrow keys to move and turn, Z to turn back, Space to drop, C to hold a shape for later and P to pause. The dotted ghost shows where the shape will land.

Skills kids practise

  • Names of 2D shapes: triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons and octagons
  • Counting sides and corners
  • How two triangles make a square
  • 3D shapes and their nets
  • Rotation and spatial reasoning
  • Planning ahead

For parents and teachers

Ask your child to name each shape and count its sides and corners before it lands. Afterwards, cut squares from paper and fold them along the diagonal to make two triangles, or cut out a cube net and fold it into a real cube. This turns the patterns from the game into something they can hold.
