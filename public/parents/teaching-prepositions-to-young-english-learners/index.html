In, On, Under: Fun Ways to Teach Prepositions to Young English Learners

By Sathish Kumar · November 21, 2026 · For ages 3-8 · 3 min read

Small words like in, on, under and behind are some of the hardest to explain. You cannot draw "on" by itself, and many languages express these ideas differently, sometimes with word endings instead of separate words. The good news is that young children learn prepositions best the same way they learn everything else: by moving, touching and playing.

Which words to teach first

Start with the place words children can see and act out:

  1. in, on, under: the core three, and easy to show with a box.
  2. next to, behind, in front of: these depend on where you are looking from, so they come next.
  3. between, above, below, near, far: these need comparing two or more things.
  4. Movement words such as into, out of, up, down, off and through: these describe a journey, not a position.

Leave time words, like "on Monday", "in July" and "at 3 o'clock", for later. They follow different rules and are easier once the place meanings are secure.

Activity 1: The toy and the box

All you need is a shoebox and a favourite soft toy. Put the toy in the box and say, "Teddy is in the box." Move it on top: "Teddy is on the box." Keep the sentence pattern exactly the same each time so only one word changes. Then swap roles: you say the sentence, and your child moves the toy. Finally, your child moves the toy and tells you where it is.

Activity 2: Body Simon Says

Children remember words they have moved to. Play "Simon says stand behind the chair", "Simon says put your hands on your head", "Simon says crawl under the table". Add movement words too: "jump into the hoop", "step out of the hoop".

Activity 3: Hide and find

Hide a small object in the room and give clues using one preposition each time: "It is under something soft." "It is next to the window." Then let your child hide it and give you the clues. Giving clues is harder than following them, so expect to help with the first few.

Activity 4: Picture talk

Picture books are full of prepositions. Pause and ask "Where is the cat?" and wait for an answer with a place word. If your child just points, model the full sentence: "Yes, the cat is under the bed."

Activity 5: Tidy-up instructions

Everyday routines are real practice. "Put your shoes next to the door." "Put the books on the shelf." "Put the blocks in the basket." Short and repeated daily, these instructions build understanding without any extra time.

Common mix-ups

  • in and on: Children often say "on the box" for both. Exaggerate the difference by opening the lid and dropping the toy inside.
  • behind and in front of: These change with your point of view. Stand side by side, facing the same way, when you teach them.
  • under and below: Both are correct in many sentences. "Under" usually means covered or touching, so start with that.

Tips for families learning English as an additional language

  • Keep using your home language too. Children who understand an idea in one language find it easier to learn the word in another.
  • Use the same simple sentence frame many times: "The ___ is ___ the ___."
  • Celebrate attempts, not just correct answers. Gently repeat the correct sentence rather than saying "no".
